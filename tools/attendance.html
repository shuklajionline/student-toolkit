Everything About College Attendance in India

Complete guide to attendance rules, state-wise variations, placement impact, and smart management strategies for Indian students.

📋

UGC 75% Attendance Mandate

The University Grants Commission (UGC) mandates minimum 75% attendance for all undergraduate and postgraduate programs across Indian universities since 2017. This applies to regular, private, and deemed universities. Falling below can result in exam detention, though most universities offer 5-10% condonation with valid medical certificates.

🏛️

State-wise Attendance Rules

  • Delhi University — 66.67% minimum
  • Mumbai University — 75% (10% condonation)
  • VTU Karnataka — 85% for professional courses
  • Anna University — 75% (10% condonation)
  • IIT Madras — 85% (very strict)
  • IIT Bombay/Delhi — 80% minimum
  • Christ University — 85% (limited condonation)
  • BITS Pilani — 80% (medical only)
🏥

Medical Condonation Policies

Most Indian universities allow 5-15% attendance relaxation for genuine medical emergencies. Required documents include registered medical practitioner certificates, hospital discharge summaries, and a formal application. Some universities like DU have specific forms. Condonation is NOT automatic — you must apply before semester exams. Sports quota and NCC participation also qualify at many institutions.

💼

Placements & Attendance Link

Many companies check attendance during campus placements. Consistent 85%+ is viewed as a sign of discipline. Some colleges require minimum 75% to sit for placements. Top recruiters like TCS, Infosys, and Wipro have been known to ask for attendance records. Poor attendance can impact your placement opportunities even if you have good grades.

Smart Attendance Tips

• Track attendance from day 1 — don't wait till semester end. • Save bunks for genuine emergencies, not casual skip days. • Use the Plan to Miss feature BEFORE bunking to check impact. • Medical certificates are your safety net — keep them ready. • Some professors track attendance separately — know your teacher's policy. • Group study sessions count as attendance in some colleges — check!
