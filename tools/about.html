The story behind

Why we built it. What we believe. What we will never do. And how our college data actually works.

Why this exists

Every resource for Indian students is either paywalled, ad-stuffed, or straight-up lying to you. Attendance calculators that ask for email before showing results. CGPA calculators with pop-ups. College rankings that charge ₹5 lakh for a "featured" slot. Resume checkers that give you a free "score" and then ask ₹2,000 to see what's wrong.

Student Toolkit was started in 2025 by an Indian college student who was tired of this. The tools on this platform should have been free to begin with. They are not complicated to build. They do not require a subscription. They were kept behind paywalls because someone figured out that students in a stressful situation will pay.

We decided not to do that.

What we will and will not do

✓ We Do

Free — permanently

Every tool, every college data point, every blog article. Free forever. No premium tier. No feature gates.

✗ We Don't

No paid rankings

Colleges cannot pay us to improve their ranking or placement figures on this platform. Not at any price.

✓ We Do

Cite our sources

Every salary figure, every placement number, every NIRF rank on Campus Insider has a data note explaining where it came from.

✗ We Don't

No sponsored content

No coaching centre partnerships. No college affiliate links. No "this article is brought to you by" disclaimers. Ever.

✓ We Do

Publish warning flags

If a college has a known issue — misleading placement averages, infrastructure problems, attendance enforcement — we say it plainly.

✗ We Don't

No login required

We do not collect your email to show you a tool result. No account needed. No data harvesting. The attendance calculator works offline.

"We know what it feels like to make a college decision with incomplete or manipulated information. We are building the platform we wish existed when we were applying. The commitment is simple: honest data, free tools, no agenda."

How our college data works

Campus Insider and the College Predictor contain data on 160+ colleges. Here is exactly how we source and present it — because you deserve to know:

Data TypeSourceOur Approach
NIRF Rankingsnirfindia.org official releasesWe show both 2024 and 2025 ranks and note year-on-year changes
Placement SalaryOfficial placement reports + Shiksha, Careers360 verified dataWe show median where published; we note when a college doesn't publish median (many IIMs don't)
FeesOfficial college fee structure pagesTotal programme fee (not per semester). We note govt vs private differences
CUET/CAT CutoffsPrevious year admission data + student-reported rangesWe show estimated ranges, not exact figures — and say so clearly
Student ReviewsAnonymous user-submitted on Campus InsiderWe do not verify claims. We moderate abuse. We disclaim on every review.
Red FlagsStudent-reported + cross-referenced with multiple sourcesWe only publish factual, documented issues — not opinions

Our ROI ranking is calculated as: average package ÷ total fees. A college with ₹8L package and ₹72K fees (SSCBS) outranks a college with ₹25L package and ₹35L fees (IIM-A) on ROI — because it does. This does not mean SSCBS is better than IIM-A. ROI is one dimension. We show multiple dimensions so you can decide.

How will this stay free?

Fair question. Student Toolkit is deliberately built lean — our infrastructure costs are kept minimal so we are never forced to monetise against our users' interests. We have no investor pressure, no growth targets, no board demanding revenue. That freedom is the whole point.

As traffic grows, we may introduce:

  • Clearly labelled sponsored college profiles — colleges that want to present their best case can pay for a featured slot, but it will say "Sponsored" prominently and our review data will not change
  • Expert resume review — AI review is free. Human review by a career counsellor could be ₹299. The AI version stays free forever.
  • An internship listing board — companies pay to list, students apply free

We will never: sell student data, run ads that clutter the experience, take money to change rankings, or put tools behind a paywall that were previously free.
