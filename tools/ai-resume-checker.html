FAQ

Everything about resume scoring

Common questions students ask about ATS and resume optimization.

What is an ATS resume checker?

An ATS (Applicant Tracking System) resume checker analyzes your resume the way automated hiring software does. It checks for keyword alignment with the job, section structure, content quality, and formatting compatibility — giving you a score and telling you exactly what to fix before submitting to employers.

How is the resume score calculated?

Your score is calculated across 7 dimensions: resume length (ideal 450–800 words), section structure, quantified achievements, action verb strength, industry keyword match for your chosen field, generic buzzword penalties, and ATS formatting safety. Each dimension contributes points toward your final score out of 100.

What is a good ATS score for a student resume?

A score of 80+ is strong and competitive — your resume will pass most ATS filters. Scores 65–79 are average and may pass automated screening but won't stand out. Below 65 is a risk zone — many ATS systems will filter your resume out before any human sees it. Aim for 80+ before submitting.

Why should I avoid buzzwords on my resume?

Phrases like "highly motivated," "team player," and "detail-oriented" appear on millions of resumes and add zero signal for recruiters. They waste space that could show real achievements. Our checker penalizes these because experienced recruiters skip past them — and so do ATS algorithms.
