Study in Australia for Indian Students 2027

Generous work hours, 2–3 year graduate visa, high visa fees.

By the Student Toolkit editorial team · Sources linked below · Editorial policy

Quick answer: Studying in Australia costs an Indian student about ₹31.5 lakh – ₹51.8 lakh per year for a master’s and ₹30.3 lakh – ₹48.9 lakh per year for a bachelor’s, including living costs. You can work 24 hours a week while studying and stay 2 years after graduation to work.

Australia student visa for Indian students

Visa & fees

Student visa (subclass 500, with CoE). A$2,000 visa application charge (from 1 July 2025).

Weeks to a few months; Genuine Student (GS) assessment applies.

Money you must show

A$29,710 for 12 months of living costs for the student, plus tuition and travel (more for family members).

Education loan sanction letters are usually accepted as proof of funds. Compare lenders in our education loan guide.

Steps

  1. Get an offer from a recognised university.
  2. Pay the deposit and receive your CoE.
  3. Arrange proof of funds and health insurance.
  4. Apply for the visa online or at VFS/embassy and give biometrics.
  5. Book accommodation and flights; complete registration after arrival.

Work during and after studies

Part-time work while studying

48 hours per fortnight during term; unlimited in scheduled course breaks.

Post-study work

Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485): 2 years for bachelor’s/master’s coursework, 3 years for PhD. Age limit 35 (with exceptions). Fee is high (reported A$4,600+); check Home Affairs.

Permanent residence

Moderate Skilled migration (points-tested 189/190/491) and employer sponsorship; occupation lists and regional study matter.

Language for jobs

English.

Things Indian students must know

  • The Genuine Student requirement replaced GTE — write a clear statement of why this course and why Australia.
  • Visa processing is slower for providers/courses with poor visa outcomes; choose institutions carefully.
  • Intakes: February — main intake, apply Aug–Nov; July — second intake, apply Feb–Apr; November — some programs.

Pros

  • 48 hrs/fortnight work during term
  • Post-study visa up to 3 years
  • High minimum wages help with living costs
  • Big Indian community; good weather

Cons

  • A$2,000 visa fee and high 485 fee
  • Caps and slower processing for some providers
  • Expensive in Sydney/Melbourne
  • Age limit 35 for 485

FAQs: studying in Australia

How much does it cost to study in Australia for Indian students?

A master’s in Australia costs about ₹31.5 lakh – ₹51.8 lakh a year including tuition, living and insurance (2 years total: ₹63.0 lakh–₹1.04 crore). A bachelor’s costs about ₹30.3 lakh – ₹48.9 lakh a year. Group of Eight (Go8) universities are at the top end. Medicine/veterinary programs cost far more.

How much money do I need to show for a Australia student visa?

A$29,710 for 12 months of living costs for the student, plus tuition and travel (more for family members).

Can Indian students work part-time in Australia?

48 hours per fortnight during term; unlimited in scheduled course breaks.

How long can I stay in Australia after graduation?

Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485): 2 years for bachelor’s/master’s coursework, 3 years for PhD. Age limit 35 (with exceptions). Fee is high (reported A$4,600+); check Home Affairs.

Can I get PR in Australia after studying?

Moderate outlook. Skilled migration (points-tested 189/190/491) and employer sponsorship; occupation lists and regional study matter.

Do I need IELTS to study in Australia?

Student visa minimum IELTS 6.0 (or equivalent); most universities ask 6.5. PTE is very popular.

What are the intakes in Australia?

February — main intake, apply Aug–Nov. July — second intake, apply Feb–Apr. November — some programs.

Which scholarships can Indian students get in Australia?

Research Training Program (RTP) (PhD/Research): Tuition offset + stipend. Destination Australia (UG/PG): A$15,000/year for regional study. University international merit awards (UG/PG): 10–50% tuition reduction is common.
