Is Your College Genuine? A 21-Point Check Before You Pay Any Fee (2027)

How to verify UGC, AICTE, NAAC, NMC and BCI approvals in 15 minutes, spot fake placement claims, avoid seat-booking scams, and get your money back if things go wrong.

The short version

  • A degree is only as good as its recognition. Check the university on UGC (and its fake-universities list), the course on the right regulator — AICTE (engineering, MBA/PGDM), NMC (medicine), BCI (law), PCI (pharmacy), INC (nursing), NCTE (B.Ed), COA (architecture).
  • Verify quality claims at the source: NAAC grade on the NAAC site, NBA for programmes, NIRF rank — and read the college’s NIRF data file for median salary and number placed.
  • Red flags: “100% placement”, “highest package” with no median, admission without the required exam, pressure to pay a “seat booking” fee today, cash-only payments, and degrees from “study centres” or off-campus centres.
  • UGC rules require a refund (minus a small processing fee) if you withdraw before the admission cut-off date it notifies — get every payment receipted.
  • Use the 21-point checklist below for every college on your list, and read our honest college pages.

Why this matters more than rankings

Every admission season, students lose fees — and sometimes years — to institutions that are unrecognised, running unapproved courses, or simply lying about placements. A degree from an unrecognised institution may not be accepted for government jobs, higher studies, professional registration (as a doctor, lawyer, engineer or pharmacist) or visas abroad.

The good news: almost everything can be verified for free, online, in about 15 minutes per college — if you know where to look. This guide shows you exactly that.

Step 1 — Is the university recognised?

In India, only institutions established by a central or state law, or declared “deemed to be universities”, can award degrees. Colleges award degrees through the university they’re affiliated to — or, if they are autonomous colleges, still in that university’s name.

  1. Find the university behind your degree. Ask: “Which university’s name will be on my degree certificate?” If the answer is vague, stop.
  2. Check it on the UGC website (ugc.gov.in) under the lists of central, state, state-private and deemed universities.
  3. Check the UGC list of fake universities. UGC publishes and updates a list of self-styled institutions that are not empowered to award degrees. Their degrees are not valid. Delhi has historically had the most entries, but they exist in several states.
  4. For affiliated colleges: check the university’s own list of affiliated colleges and the courses each is permitted to run — affiliation is course-specific.
  5. For deemed universities: confirm the campus you’re joining is an approved campus. Off-campus centres need separate approval.
Distance and online degrees

Open and distance learning (ODL) and online degrees are valid only from institutions and programmes recognised by UGC’s Distance Education Bureau (deb.ugc.ac.in) for that academic year. Many “study centres” and franchise operators sell degrees from universities that are not approved for that mode or that course. Technical courses like B.Tech cannot be done through distance mode.

PGDM vs MBA

An MBA is a university degree. A PGDM is a diploma from an AICTE-approved autonomous institute. Good PGDMs (IIMs historically, XLRI, SPJIMR, MDI and others) are highly valued. For government jobs and PhD admissions, check whether the PGDM has AIU equivalence to an MBA. Compare honestly with our BBA & MBA tier list.

Step 3 — Verify quality claims: NAAC, NBA, NIRF

NAAC accreditation

NAAC grades institutions (A++, A+, A, B++, B+, B, C). Colleges love to print “NAAC A++” on hoardings — verify it on the NAAC website: the grade, the validity period (accreditation expires) and that it’s for this institution, not a sister campus. NAAC has been moving to a revised framework, so you may also see newer accreditation formats.

NBA accreditation

NBA accredits programmes (e.g., B.Tech Mechanical), not whole colleges. A college may have NBA for two branches and not for the one you’re joining.

NIRF — and the data file most students never read

The Ministry of Education’s NIRF rankings are useful, but the real gold is each ranked institution’s NIRF data submission (a PDF linked from the NIRF site and the college website). It shows, for recent batches:

  • Number of students admitted, graduated in minimum time, placed, and going to higher studies.
  • Median salary of placed graduates.
  • Faculty numbers, PhD faculty, research spending.

Compare the college’s brochure claims against its own NIRF data. If the brochure says “average package ₹12 LPA” and the NIRF median is ₹4.5 LPA, you know which number to trust.

Step 4 — Decode placement claims

Misleading

  • “Highest package ₹45 LPA” (one student, often international or pre-placement)
  • “100% placement” (counting 1-month internships, family business, or students who opted out)
  • “500+ recruiters visited” (most may hire zero)
  • Logos of Google, Amazon, Deloitte on the banner

Meaningful

  • Median salary for your specific course and batch
  • % of eligible students placed, and how many opted out
  • An audited placement report (top B-schools follow Indian Placement Reporting Standards)
  • Names of seniors placed, verifiable on LinkedIn
The LinkedIn test

Search LinkedIn for “[College name] [course] [graduation year]”. Look at where 20–30 recent graduates actually work. This takes 10 minutes and beats any brochure. Then check what their likely salaries mean in hand with our salary calculator.

15 red flags of a fake or predatory college

  1. Not on the UGC university lists — or on the fake-universities list.
  2. Course not in the regulator’s approved list for this year (AICTE, NMC, BCI, PCI, INC, NCTE, COA).
  3. “Admission without entrance exam” for courses that legally require one (NEET for MBBS/BDS; CLAT or state tests where mandated).
  4. Pressure: “Only 3 seats left — pay ₹50,000 today to block your seat.”
  5. Requests for cash, or payment to a personal account or an agent’s account.
  6. “Management quota” or “donation” sold outside official counselling.
  7. “100% placement guarantee” or salary promises in writing from an agent.
  8. NAAC/NIRF claims that don’t match the official websites.
  9. Degree from a different university than the one advertised, or a “certificate” passed off as a degree.
  10. “International degree” through a tie-up you can’t verify on the foreign university’s own site.
  11. Distance/online degrees for courses or in years not approved by UGC-DEB.
  12. No clear fee structure, refund policy or hostel fee in writing.
  13. Original certificates retained by the college as “security”.
  14. Website with stock photos, no faculty names or qualifications, and no physical address.
  15. Reviews that are all 5-star, posted in bursts, with no detail.

Fees, refunds and getting your documents back

  • Refunds: UGC issues a fee refund policy for each academic year. It requires a full refund — minus a small processing fee (capped at ₹1,000 in recent notices) — if you cancel admission before the cut-off date UGC notifies, and a graded refund after that. AICTE and state counselling bodies have their own rules for their admissions.
  • Original certificates: institutions should not retain your original documents. Submit attested copies and show originals for verification.
  • Get everything in writing: fee receipts, refund policy, scholarship or fee-waiver promises, hostel terms.

Where to complain if something goes wrong

  1. Write to the institution first — email the registrar/principal with dates, receipts and what you want (refund, documents). Keep it factual. Our letter writer can help you draft it.
  2. University grievance cell / ombudsperson: under UGC’s 2023 regulations on redress of student grievances, universities must have grievance redressal committees and an ombudsperson.
  3. UGC e-Samadhan portal for university-related complaints; AICTE’s grievance portal for technical institutions.
  4. Consumer forum: for fee refund and deficiency-of-service disputes, students have approached consumer commissions.
  5. Police / cyber crime (cybercrime.gov.in) for outright fraud — fake admissions, fake universities, payment scams.

Frequently asked questions

How do I check if a university is fake in India?

Check the university on the UGC website’s lists of central, state, state-private and deemed universities, and check UGC’s published list of fake universities. A fake university cannot award valid degrees.

How can I check if a college is AICTE approved?

Search the institution on AICTE’s approved-institutes list for the current academic year and confirm that your specific course and intake are listed. Approval is renewed every year.

How do I verify a college’s NAAC grade?

Search the institution on the NAAC website and check the grade and the accreditation validity dates. Make sure the grade belongs to the same institution and campus, not a sister institution.

Is a PGDM equal to an MBA?

A PGDM is a diploma from an AICTE-approved autonomous institute, while an MBA is a university degree. Employers value good PGDMs highly. For government jobs and further studies, check whether the PGDM has AIU equivalence to an MBA.

Will I get my fees back if I cancel admission?

UGC’s refund policy requires a full refund, minus a small processing fee, if you withdraw before the cut-off date UGC notifies for that academic year, and partial refunds after that. Keep receipts and request the refund in writing.

Are online degrees valid in India?

Online and distance degrees are valid only when both the institution and the programme are recognised by UGC’s Distance Education Bureau for that year. Technical programmes like B.Tech cannot be taken through distance mode.
