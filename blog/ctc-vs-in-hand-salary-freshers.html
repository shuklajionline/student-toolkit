CTC vs In-Hand Salary: How to Read Your First Job Offer (2027)

Why “₹12 LPA” rarely means ₹1 lakh a month. Every line of an Indian salary breakup explained — PF, gratuity, variable pay, bonuses, tax — with worked examples and the questions to ask before you sign.

The short version

  • CTC (Cost to Company) is everything the employer spends on you in a year — including money you never see monthly, like employer PF, gratuity, insurance and variable pay.
  • In-hand = Gross salary − employee PF − professional tax − income tax. Variable pay, joining bonus and retention bonus are paid separately and are often conditional.
  • Under the new tax regime (Budget 2025 slabs), salary up to about ₹12.75 lakh in a year can be tax-free thanks to the ₹75,000 standard deduction and the rebate.
  • Worked examples: ₹6 LPA → about ₹45,000/month; ₹12 LPA (10% variable) → about ₹79,400/month + variable; ₹18 LPA → about ₹1.11 lakh/month + variable.
  • Before signing, ask for the fixed vs variable split, bonus clawback terms, bond/notice period, and whether PF is on full basic or capped.

What CTC actually includes

Indian offer letters quote salary as CTC — Cost to Company: the total annual amount a company spends on employing you. It is a budgeting number for the employer, not a promise of monthly cash. A typical fresher CTC has three layers:

LayerComponentsDo you get it monthly?
Fixed gross salaryBasic, HRA, special allowance, LTA, other allowancesYes — minus deductions
Employer contributions & benefitsEmployer PF (12% of basic or of the PF wage ceiling), gratuity provision (≈4.81% of basic), group health/term insurance premium, meal cardsNo — saved for you or spent on your behalf
Variable & one-timePerformance bonus/variable pay, joining bonus, relocation, retention bonus, ESOPs/RSUsNo — paid once or yearly, often conditional
Key idea

Two offers with the same CTC can differ by ₹10,000+ per month in hand, depending on how much is variable, whether PF is capped, and how much is “benefits”.

Every salary component, explained

Basic salary

The foundation of your pay, usually 35–50% of fixed salary. PF and gratuity are calculated on basic, so a higher basic means more retirement savings but slightly lower monthly in-hand.

New Labour Codes

The four Labour Codes, which the government brought into force from 21 November 2025, define “wages” (basic + dearness allowance + retaining allowance) as at least 50% of total pay. Many companies are restructuring salaries so basic is around half — which raises PF and gratuity and can reduce monthly in-hand. They also make fixed-term employees eligible for gratuity after one year. Rules are being implemented in stages, so check your offer’s actual breakup.

HRA (House Rent Allowance)

Usually 40–50% of basic. Under the old tax regime you can claim HRA exemption if you pay rent. Under the new regime (the default), HRA is fully taxable — but most freshers pay zero tax anyway at lower salaries.

Special allowance / flexible pay

The balancing figure that makes the numbers add up. Fully taxable. Some companies let you split it into meal cards, fuel, phone and books reimbursements.

Provident Fund (PF / EPF)

  • Employee PF: 12% of basic, deducted from your salary every month. It’s your savings — it earns interest (8.25% for FY 2024-25 as declared by EPFO) and can be withdrawn under rules.
  • Employer PF: another 12% — but it’s already inside your CTC. Of this, up to ₹1,250/month goes to the pension scheme (EPS).
  • Capped vs full: PF is mandatory on basic up to ₹15,000/month. Many companies cap PF at ₹1,800/month (12% of ₹15,000); others contribute on full basic. Capped PF = higher in-hand; full PF = more savings.

Gratuity

A lump sum paid when you leave after the qualifying period (traditionally 5 years of continuous service). Companies often show ≈4.81% of basic in CTC as a gratuity provision. If you leave in 2 years, you usually never receive that part — so treat it as “not real money” for a fresher.

Professional tax

A state tax on employment, at most ₹2,500 a year. Karnataka, Maharashtra, West Bengal, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat and others levy it; Delhi, Haryana and Uttar Pradesh do not. Typically about ₹200 a month.

Insurance

Group health insurance premiums are often included in CTC. Useful, but it doesn’t reach your bank account.

Variable pay, bonuses and the fine print

Variable / performance pay

Paid yearly or quarterly based on your rating and company performance. A “10% variable” often pays out at 60–100% of target. Ask what the average payout was last year.

Joining bonus

Paid once, usually with the first or second salary. Almost always has a clawback: leave within 12 months and you repay it (sometimes pro-rata).

Retention bonus

Paid after you complete 12–24 months. If you leave earlier, you get nothing. Some offers inflate CTC with it.

ESOPs / RSUs

Shares that “vest” over 3–4 years. Valuable at listed or well-funded companies, uncertain at early startups. They are rarely cash in year one.

CTC inflation trick

Some offers quote “₹10 LPA” that includes a ₹1 lakh joining bonus, ₹1.5 lakh variable and ₹50,000 of insurance and gratuity. The real fixed pay is ₹7 lakh. Always ask: “What is my fixed annual gross, and what is my monthly in-hand?”

Income tax for freshers (new regime)

The new tax regime is the default. Budget 2025 set these slabs, which apply to salary income unless a later Budget changes them:

Taxable income (per year)Rate
Up to ₹4,00,000Nil
₹4,00,001 – ₹8,00,0005%
₹8,00,001 – ₹12,00,00010%
₹12,00,001 – ₹16,00,00015%
₹16,00,001 – ₹20,00,00020%
₹20,00,001 – ₹24,00,00025%
Above ₹24,00,00030%
  • Standard deduction: ₹75,000 for salaried people in the new regime.
  • Rebate: if taxable income is up to ₹12 lakh, tax is nil. So gross salary up to about ₹12.75 lakh pays no income tax. Just above the limit, marginal relief applies so you don’t pay more tax than the extra income.
  • Cess: 4% health & education cess on the tax.
  • Old regime: lower slabs’ benefits are smaller, but you can claim HRA, 80C (PF, ELSS, insurance), 80D (health insurance), 80E (education-loan interest) and more. Freshers with a large education loan or high rent sometimes benefit — compare both in the calculator.

The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026 and renumbered many sections; check the current Budget for any slab changes before filing.

Comparing two offers properly

  1. Compare fixed in-hand first. That pays rent and EMIs. Variable is a bonus, not a salary.
  2. Discount uncertain money. Count variable at its usual payout (e.g., 70%), joining bonus as conditional, ESOPs at near-zero for early startups.
  3. Adjust for city cost. ₹8 LPA in Pune or Jaipur can beat ₹10 LPA in Mumbai or Bengaluru after rent. Check rent for a shared flat near the office.
  4. Weigh learning and brand. Your first job’s skills and name drive your second salary — often a 30–60% jump when you switch after 1.5–3 years.
  5. Check bonds and notice period. A ₹2 lakh service bond or 90-day notice period limits your options.

10 questions to ask HR before you accept

1. What is my fixed annual gross and expected monthly in-hand?
2. How much of the CTC is variable, how is it calculated, and what was last year's average payout?
3. Is PF contributed on full basic or capped at Rs 15,000 wage?
4. Is gratuity, insurance or any one-time bonus included in the CTC figure?
5. What are the clawback terms for the joining bonus?
6. Is there a service bond or training-cost recovery? For how long and how much?
7. What is the notice period during and after probation?
8. When is the first appraisal, and what was the typical increment last cycle?
9. Is relocation or accommodation provided for the first weeks?
10. Which city and office will I be posted to, and is the role hybrid or on-site?

Can a fresher negotiate salary?

On campus mass hiring (IT services, large banks), packages are fixed and negotiation rarely works. Off-campus, at startups and for specialised roles, polite negotiation is normal — especially if you have a competing offer, a strong internship, or a rare skill.

  • Negotiate the fixed component, joining bonus or relocation — not just CTC.
  • Use a specific, honest anchor: “I have an offer at ₹X fixed. I’d prefer to join you — can you match the fixed pay?”
  • Never fake an offer. Recruiters talk to each other and verify.

Your first salary: a simple money plan

  1. Emergency fund: build 3 months of expenses in a savings account or liquid fund before anything else.
  2. Health insurance: the company policy covers you while employed; consider a small personal policy you keep across jobs.
  3. Repay high-interest debt: credit-card balances first, then personal loans. Education loans are cheaper — and under the old regime, their interest is deductible.
  4. Automate investing: a monthly SIP in a diversified index fund after the emergency fund is built.
  5. Avoid lifestyle creep: phones on EMI and “buy now pay later” eat first salaries fast.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between CTC and in-hand salary?

CTC is the total amount a company spends on you in a year, including employer PF, gratuity, insurance and variable pay. In-hand salary is what reaches your bank account each month after employee PF, professional tax and income tax are deducted from gross salary.

How much is 12 LPA in hand per month?

It depends on the structure. With 10% variable pay, PF on full basic and the new tax regime, a ₹12 LPA offer gives roughly ₹79,000 a month in hand plus the variable pay once a year. If everything is fixed and PF is capped at ₹1,800 a month, it is around ₹93,000.

Is gratuity part of in-hand salary?

No. Gratuity is paid as a lump sum when you leave after completing the qualifying service, traditionally five years. If you leave earlier, you usually do not receive the gratuity shown in your CTC.

Is salary up to 12 lakh tax-free?

Under the new tax regime with Budget 2025 slabs, taxable income up to ₹12 lakh gets a full rebate. Adding the ₹75,000 standard deduction, gross salary up to about ₹12.75 lakh pays no income tax. Check the latest Budget for changes.

Should a fresher choose the old or new tax regime?

Most freshers pay less tax in the new regime. The old regime can be better if you have large deductions such as high rent with HRA, 80C investments, health insurance and education-loan interest. Compare both before you submit your declaration to HR.

What happens to my joining bonus if I resign early?

Most offer letters require you to repay the joining bonus, fully or pro-rata, if you leave within a set period, usually 12 months. Read the clawback clause before you accept.
