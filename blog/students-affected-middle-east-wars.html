Education Under Fire: How Wars Are Destroying a Generation of Students

March 6, 2026 • 16 min read • Global Education

Across the Middle East, war has turned classrooms into rubble and universities into empty shells. From Gaza to Yemen to Syria, millions of students have lost access to education, creating what experts now describe as a lost generation.

Gaza: Collapse of an Entire Education System

Since October 2023 the war in Gaza has devastated the entire education system. According to humanitarian reports, over 12,800 students have been killed and hundreds of schools have been destroyed.

All 19 universities in Gaza have suffered major destruction or closure. Libraries, laboratories, and research centers have been reduced to rubble, leaving more than 600,000 students without access to formal education.

Experts now use the term "scholasticide" to describe the systematic destruction of education infrastructure in the region.

Yemen: A Decade of Lost Classrooms

Yemen's civil war has lasted more than a decade and created one of the world's largest education crises. UNICEF estimates that 4.5 million children are currently out of school.

More than 2,900 schools have been destroyed, damaged, or used as shelters or military positions. Many teachers have gone unpaid for years, forcing schools to close permanently.

For millions of Yemeni students, education has become a distant dream rather than a daily routine.

Syria: Displaced Students Across Borders

The Syrian civil war has displaced over six million people internationally. Among them are millions of students who have lost access to universities and schools.

Research from international organizations shows that many refugee children struggle with literacy and numeracy after years of interrupted education.

Even students who reach university age often face barriers such as legal restrictions, tuition costs, and discrimination in host countries.

Iraq: Universities Caught in Conflict

During the fight against ISIS, several Iraqi universities were severely damaged. Mosul University — one of the country's most historic institutions — suffered massive destruction during the battle for the city.

Even after the conflict ended, unexploded devices left on campuses continued to threaten students returning to study.

Lebanon: A Crisis Built on Crisis

Lebanon's education system has faced years of economic collapse, refugee pressure, and political instability. Studies suggest that students in Lebanon have lost nearly 60% of classroom learning time over the past decade.

Schools frequently close due to funding shortages, strikes, and displacement caused by regional conflict.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many students have been killed in Gaza since 2023?

Humanitarian organizations estimate that more than 12,800 students have died since the escalation of conflict in October 2023.

How many children are out of school in Yemen?

UNICEF estimates that roughly 4.5 million Yemeni children are currently out of school due to war and economic collapse.

Why does war affect education so heavily?

War destroys school infrastructure, displaces families, disrupts funding, and often forces children into labor or survival situations instead of education.

Conclusion

Behind every statistic is a student who once had dreams of a career, a classroom, and a future. Wars in Gaza, Yemen, Syria, Iraq, and Lebanon have disrupted the education of millions of young people.

Protecting education in conflict zones is not only a humanitarian issue but also a global responsibility. Without access to learning, entire generations risk losing the opportunity to rebuild their societies.
